Summers at my grandmother's house

by Carlos Vera

When I was a child, my family lived in a big city in the north of Chile. Life there was busy, and my parents worked a lot. Every summer, from December to March, my brother and I went to stay with my grandmother in a small village near the sea. I was always very happy there because I could play outside all day.

Our days used to start early. My grandmother woke up at six and made fresh bread in her little kitchen. The smell was wonderful, and my brother and I used to run to the table for breakfast. After that, we used to help her in the garden. She grew tomatoes, potatoes and flowers. I didn't like working in the garden at first, but she always said, "Do it with a smile, and it's easy."

In the afternoon, we used to go to the river with the other children from the village. We didn't have phones or tablets, so we played football, swam and looked for small fish. I used to stay in the water for hours! At night, we used to sit outside, and my grandmother told us stories about her life. I didn't use to sleep well in the city, but in her house I always slept very well.

Everything changed when I was fifteen. My grandmother got sick and moved to our house in the city. The village house was empty, and we stopped going in the summer. At first, I felt sad because I missed the river and the quiet nights. Now I'm thirty-five, and I live in a different city. Every summer, I take my own children to the village. The house is old, but the river is the same. We still make bread in the kitchen, and I tell them the same stories.
