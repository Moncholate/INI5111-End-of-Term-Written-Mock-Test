A New Life in Madrid

by Lucas Mendoza

When my family moved to Madrid, Spain, I was fourteen years old. My new school was enormous — more than 800 students and 35 children in each class. It was always very noisy, and the cold weather was hard for me too. At lunchtime, all the students ate together in a big cafeteria. I used to feel very lost there because I didn't know where to sit or who to talk to. Everything felt confusing and a little scary.


Back in Ecuador, where I grew up, life was very different. My school was small — only 20 students in my class and I knew everyone very well. My teachers had known me for years and I was good at most subjects. I used to feel very confident and comfortable at school. In my free time, I used to play football on the beach with my friends almost every afternoon. We also used to go swimming in the sea after school. I loved living there.


Not everything in Madrid was difficult, though. I used to enjoy Physical Education class a lot because the teacher was very kind and encouraging. She noticed that I was good at sports and invited me to join the school basketball team. That helped me make new friends and I started to feel better about my new life. I also liked my Spanish Literature class very much. I was already a good reader and the teacher always gave me very positive marks on my written work. Those two subjects really helped me feel at home.

1. How was Lucas's new school in Madrid? Give two details. 1 pt
2. Why did Lucas use to feel lost at lunchtime in Madrid? 1 pt
3. Describe Lucas's school in Ecuador. Why did he feel comfortable there? 1 pt
4. Name two activities Lucas used to do with his friends in Ecuador. 1 pt
5. How did Lucas's PE teacher help him at his new school in Madrid? 1 pt
6. Why was Lucas good at Spanish Literature? 1 pt

Tips (a–g)

a) You should try to study a little every day instead of studying everything at once. Make sure you take short breaks and try to go to bed at a regular time. A good night's sleep before an exam is very important. Don't forget to eat well too.
b) You should start with small changes. Try to walk for 30 minutes every day and drink more water. You don't need to go to a gym — just moving more is a great start. You should also try to eat more fruit and vegetables every day.
c) You should be patient with yourself — it is normal to feel nervous at the beginning. Try to smile and say hello to your colleagues every day. You should also ask questions when you don't understand something. People are usually happy to help new team members.
d) You should talk to your friend as soon as possible. Send them a message and tell them you want to fix things. It is important to listen to their feelings too, not only yours. A good friendship is worth a difficult conversation.
e) You should start with simple recipes and practice every weekend. Try to cook different things — pasta, rice, soup. Cooking at home is cheaper and healthier than eating out. You can also watch videos online to learn new techniques.
f) You should check all the places you visited yesterday. If you can't find it, call your phone from another phone — someone may answer. You should also tell your family so they know how to contact you. Try not to worry too much.
g) You should try to practice a little every day, even just for 15 minutes. Use an app on your computer or watch short videos in that language. You don't need hours of free time — small moments every day can help a lot. The most important thing is to be consistent.
